The training pipeline
1 · data
Generate a document from a recipe I control
Pick a category. Draw 6–14 words: with probability 0.20 from that category's signature words, otherwise uniformly from the whole 40-word vocabulary.
“my bill was charged twice please help”  →  billing
↓
2 · target
Compute the exact answer — as a probability
Because I chose the recipe, Bayes' rule gives the true P(category | document) in closed form. That distribution is the training target, not the label.
billing 0.62   technical 0.19   sales 0.12   spam 0.07
↓
3 · train
Score the options, compare to the truth
The model turns its scores into probabilities. The loss is how far those sit from the true ones, plus a Brier term that rewards being right and honest about it.
loss = crossentropy(true, predicted)
       + 0.3 × brier(predicted)
20,000 documents10 epochsAdamW, one-cyclebatch 12840 s on a laptop CPU
↓
4 · test
5,000 held-out documents — and a known ceiling
Measure the answer it picks, how far its confidence sits from the truth, and how often its stated probabilities match what actually happens. The recipe also gives the best score anyone could get on this task, so "good" has a number.
